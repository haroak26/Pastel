# Cursor design language

## When to reach for this reference
Use Cursor's design language for developer tools, AI coding products, and technical products for professional builders — anything that must feel precise, calm, and engineering-led. It suits light, low-contrast surfaces with quiet warmth and one strong accent. Ideal for audiences who judge a tool by its latency, not its decorations.

## Brand personality
Cursor is the focused craftsman: quiet, warm, and deeply technical. The interface gets out of the way so the product's capability can speak, using low-contrast warm grays and restrained typography to feel calm at scale. It is confident without bravado — the design equivalent of a well-machined tool that does not need to announce itself. There is no whimsy, only precision.

## Color philosophy
The palette is warm and muted: page `#F8F8F5`, panels `#F1EFEC`, ink `#20201D`, and muted text `#6F706C` — a lightly textured, low-contrast world where pure bright white is avoided except on media frames. Hairline rules `#DFDDD8` separate content. A single orange `#E97745` is the entire color story: links, the one strong accent, and focus states. Everything else stays in the warm gray family.

## Typography approach
A quiet grotesk (Inter-style) with understated character: primary headlines at 46px, weight 400, line-height 1.0 and tight tracking; feature headings 22–32px; body 12–14px; and tiny 9–11px captions for dates and metadata. The hierarchy is real but never loud — size differences communicate, and text carries the whole personality of the surface.

## Spacing & density
Generous editorial whitespace on a 4px scale: sections breathe with 48–120px vertical spacing, and the content rail uses 16px gutters on mobile and 32–64px on desktop, capped at 1180px. Density is medium — roomy but not empty. The rhythm is consistent so scanning feels effortless.

## Corner radius & shape language
Very quiet radii: 3px on small elements, 6px standard, 10px on the largest frames, and full pills reserved for the few circular controls. Corners are present but barely noticeable — enough to soften, never enough to feel friendly or toy-like. The overall silhouette is rectangular and tool-like.

## Elevation & depth
Flat, with texture instead of shadow. Surfaces separate by warm tone shifts (panel on page) and 1px rules, not drop shadows. The only depth lives inside framed product media — screenshots presented in pale rectangles with soft shadows — and even those stay subtle. The page itself never lifts.

## Iconography & imagery
Icons are minimal, thin, and functional; they support labels rather than replace them. Imagery policy is media-within-frames: product surfaces live inside pale gray or warm-gray rectangles, clearly framed and set apart from the design system. Frames scale no more than 1% on hover — media is content, not decoration.

## Signature patterns
- **Orange as a link-first accent** — the single accent lives in text links that underline on hover, not in fills
- **Framed media** — every product surface is presented inside a quiet pale frame, set apart from the interface
- **Paired CTA hierarchy** — one dark filled control next to one pale outlined control, always
- **Tiny metadata captions** — 9–11px dates and category labels carry scannability without noise
- **Tone-shift surfaces** — panels differ by warmth and lightness, not borders or shadows
- **Underline-before-motion** — hover changes color or underline first; only frames get a capped 1% scale

## Motion philosophy
Extremely restrained. Hover is color and underline changes; the one allowed scale motion is capped at 1% and feels like nothing at all. Transitions are fast and dry. The motion model: the product is static, and only the user's attention should move.

## Voice & copy tone
Quiet, precise, and product-first. Copy names capabilities plainly and lets the product prove them; captions and labels are terse. The tone is that of an engineer writing for engineers — no hype, no exclamation marks, no invented marketing claims. Confidence is expressed through understatement.

- Do not copy Cursor's UI copy or interaction patterns verbatim
