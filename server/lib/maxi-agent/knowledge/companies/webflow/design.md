# Webflow design language

## When to reach for this reference
Builder/creator tools, no-code platforms, and products where technical capability and approachable design must coexist. Fits sites that want to convey "powerful but accessible."

## Brand personality
Structured, approachable, confident, builder-focused, modern.

## Color philosophy
A restrained palette built around a confident blue-violet accent against a clean white or very dark base. One accent carries the UI — applied to primary CTAs, active states, and selected items — while the rest of the interface stays neutral. Gradients appear only as a deliberate signature element (like the Webflow logo), not scattered throughout the interface. The overall feel is technical credibility with creative warmth, not cold enterprise software.

## Typography approach
A single geometric sans-serif workhorse family used throughout the entire product. Type sizes are spacious and confident — headings are substantially larger than body text, creating clear hierarchy through scale alone rather than through weight mixing or decoration. Body text is comfortable at 16px+, and labels in the builder canvas stay crisp and small without feeling cramped. Monospace is reserved for code snippets and property values.

## Spacing & density
Clean, airy spacing that puts white space around builder tooling so the canvas breathes. Toolbars and property panels are dense where needed (the builder interface), but marketing surfaces, onboarding, and documentation use generous whitespace. The rhythm alternates between tight tool surfaces and open canvas areas — the contrast between the two densities makes each read correctly.

## Corner radius & shape language
Moderate rounding (6–10px) across most UI — soft enough to feel modern and approachable, but not so round it reads as playful. Buttons, inputs, and cards share a consistent radius value. The relationship between the rectilinear builder canvas and the rounded UI chrome creates a deliberate, pleasant tension.

## Elevation & depth
Mostly flat with light borders carrying separation. Shadows are reserved for genuinely floating elements — dropdowns, modals, context menus, and the occasional elevated card in a complex editor layout. The canvas itself stays completely flat; it's the tooling panels that use subtle elevation to separate themselves from the work surface.

## Iconography & imagery
A clean, geometric icon set with consistent stroke weights — icons feel precise and builder-appropriate. Product imagery and templates are shown cleanly with subtle device chrome. The overall visual approach is to let user work (templates, sites, designs) be the hero imagery rather than decorative illustrations.

## Signature patterns
1. **Split builder view** — a clean left/right or top/bottom split between tooling panels and a full-bleed canvas preview, with the canvas framed like a genuine device viewport.
2. **Template cards with subtle depth** — templates shown in preview cards that use soft elevation and device-chrome framing to make the output feel real before it's built.
3. **Property panels with clear grouping** — dense but legible sidebar panels where related properties cluster under clear headings with hairline dividers, never walls of undifferentiated inputs.

## Motion philosophy
Snappy, responsive transitions that feel like a native application — 150–200ms micro-interactions for state changes, smooth canvas zoom and pan, and subtle hover lifts on interactive template previews. Motion gives feedback on builder actions without ever feeling decorative or slow.

## Voice & copy tone
Confident, capable, and slightly technical — the voice of someone who knows their craft and wants to hand you the tools, not sell you a dream. Sentences are direct and action-oriented. Error messages and tooltips explain what happened and what to do next in plain terms. Never condescending, never overly casual.
