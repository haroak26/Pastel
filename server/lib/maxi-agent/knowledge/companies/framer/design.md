# Framer design language

## When to reach for this reference
Use this reference when designing creative tools, website builders, portfolio platforms, design portfolio sites, or any product that serves designers and creative professionals who expect the tool itself to be beautifully crafted. Framer's language is ideal for products where the UI is part of the proof of quality — if the tool looks good, users trust it to make good things.

## Brand personality
Framer exudes design-tool cool — the confident, taste-driven personality of a product made by designers, for designers. It is opinionated about aesthetics without being exclusionary. The personality blends three qualities: the precision of a professional tool, the expressiveness of a creative canvas, and the aspirational cool of a design-forward brand. There is an undercurrent of "if you know, you know" — the interface is not obvious to everyone, and that is intentional. It prioritizes the experience of the skilled user over the comprehension of the first-time visitor. The personality says "this is what good design looks like; now go make something."

## Color philosophy
Gradient accents are the signature — not full-surface washes like Arc, but precise, jewel-like gradients used as highlights: a button gradient, a selected-state gradient, a section-divider gradient, a hover-state gradient. The palette is synthetic, saturated, and beautiful: violet-to-blue, blue-to-teal, coral-to-pink. The gradients feel technical rather than atmospheric — sharp, clean color transitions with tight stops. Beyond the accents, the interface is nearly monochrome: a dark gray or off-black canvas (`#111111` or `#1A1A1A`), slightly lighter panels (`#222222` or `#262626`), and white or light gray type. The contrast between the restrained chrome and the vivid gradient accents is the core color dynamic. Light mode inverts to white/light gray chrome with gradient accents that pop even harder.

## Typography approach
Type is precise and contemporary. A single geometric sans-serif (Inter, SF Pro, or a custom design-forward sans) is used throughout — clean, efficient, and highly legible at UI sizes. The type system runs at 13px for panel labels and metadata, 14px for body, 16–20px for headings. Line-height is tight (1.3–1.4) in panels, slightly more generous (1.5) in the canvas and preview areas. Weight range is narrow: Regular (400) and Medium/Semibold (500–600) cover the UI. The canvas — where users design — has full typographic freedom; the UI around it is rigorously controlled. If the product renders user-created content (websites, prototypes), the type system there is entirely the user's domain.

## Spacing & density
Framer uses a two-level density strategy. The UI panels (layers, properties, assets) are dense and efficient with 8px-grid alignment, 12–16px padding, and compact controls — designers expect tool UIs to be information-rich and fast to navigate. The canvas area, by contrast, is open and unconstrained — it is the user's creative space and the UI recedes completely. This density contrast is intentional: tight where the user configures, open where the user creates. Panel widths are standard (240–280px for sidebars, 200–240px for property panels). Between panels, 0px gaps with 1px borders maintain the clean, seamed look that design tools favor.

## Corner radius & shape language
Precise and contemporary: 8–10px on panels and modals, 6px on buttons, 4px on inputs and controls, 12–16px on floating UI elements and popovers. The shape language is geometric and refined — not as soft as Arc, not as sharp as Superhuman. The canvas elements (frames, components, shapes) use a distinct selection-handle system: small squares or circles at corners with a bounding-box outline in the accent color, creating the visual language of "editable, movable, transformable." This is a design-tool shape language: everything in the UI is rectilinear and efficient; everything on the canvas is malleable and expressive. The gradient accent bar at the top of modals or selected panels — a thin 2–3px gradient strip — is a subtle Framer shape signature.

## Elevation & depth
Controlled and minimal. The dark canvas background serves as the baseline; panels sit on a slightly lighter surface with no shadow — just a 1px border on the inner edge. Modals, dropdowns, and popovers use a crisp 0px-4px-12px shadow at 20–30% opacity with a subtle 1px border. The floating toolbar or properties panel that follows the selection uses a tighter shadow to create a sense of attachment to the selected element. The overall approach: flat when possible, softly elevated when necessary. Depth is a tool for managing attention, not for creating spatial realism.

## Iconography & imagery
Icons are precise and design-forward — drawn at 16–18px with 1.5px strokes, consistent cap style, and a refined visual weight. The icon set should feel curated and designer-approved (Phosphor, Lucide, or a custom set with consistent geometric construction). Imagery is central to Framer's brand because the product is about creating visual content. The marketing site, templates, and showcase are rich with high-quality screenshots of websites, prototypes, and designs — presented beautifully in device mockups, with soft shadows and considered composition. Template thumbnails and project covers use rich, design-forward imagery. But the UI chrome itself is free of decorative imagery — the visual richness comes from the user's own work on the canvas.

## Signature patterns
The canvas-and-panels layout: a central infinite canvas surrounded by collapsible tool and property panels. The floating design toolbar that appears contextually near the selection. The drag-and-drop component insertion with live preview. The "Remix" template system where users start from a pre-designed, fully-functional template rather than a blank canvas. The responsive breakpoint controls with a scrubber-like slider. The properties panel that morphs its contents based on selection type — text properties for text, layout properties for frames, effect properties for layers. The publish/preview flow that transitions from the editor to a full-screen preview of the live site. The gradient accent strip that appears on selected elements, active panels, and primary CTAs.

## Motion philosophy
Motion-forward — this is a product that treats animation and interactivity as first-class citizens. The tool UI uses crisp, responsive transitions (150–200ms) with subtle ease-out curves. But the canvas is where motion shines: Framer's core value proposition includes prototyping interactive animations. Page transitions within the editor, component insertions, drag-and-drop operations, and canvas zoom/pan all use smooth, physical-feeling animations with spring physics. The gradient accent strip can subtly shift hue on hover or selection. The overall motion philosophy: the UI responds crisply; the canvas comes alive. Animation is not decoration — it is a demonstration of the product's capabilities. The tool should feel so responsive that using it is inherently satisfying, independent of what is being made.

## Voice & copy tone
Design-forward, confident, and slightly aspirational. Framer's copy speaks to designers in their language — it references "pixels," "breakpoints," "interactions," "components" — and assumes a baseline of design literacy. It is concise and direct, never over-explaining. Marketing copy is more expressive: "Design and publish sites you are proud of," "The internet is your canvas." The product copy in the UI is minimal — icons and short labels carry the interface; sentences are rare outside of onboarding tooltips and documentation. The overall tone: "You are a designer. You know what you are doing. Here is an incredibly powerful tool. Go." Sentence case throughout. No exclamation marks in the product UI.
