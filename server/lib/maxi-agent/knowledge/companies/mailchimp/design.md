# Mailchimp design language

## When to reach for this reference
Use this reference when designing marketing tools, creative platforms, email builders, or any product that serves small business owners and creators who may not be technically sophisticated. Mailchimp's language is ideal for interfaces that need to feel accessible, encouraging, and personality-forward while still supporting complex workflows like campaign management, automation builders, and audience segmentation.

## Brand personality
Mailchimp is the quirky, encouraging friend who also happens to be extremely competent at marketing. The personality is defined by warmth, optimism, and a distinctive sense of humor — but crucially, the quirkiness never compromises usability. It is approachable without being condescending, creative without being chaotic, and playful without being unprofessional. Underneath the personality is a surprising depth of power; the design language makes complex marketing operations feel manageable and even fun. The Freddie mascot anchors the brand's friendly, slightly irreverent character.

## Color philosophy
Warm yellow (`#FFE01B`) is the unmistakable brand anchor — used as a background, an accent, and a brand signifier. It is paired with near-black (`#241C15`) for type and primary UI elements, creating a high-contrast, energetic foundation. The yellow is warm and optimistic, never acidic or neon. Secondary palettes include soft peaches, warm greens, and muted teals — all warm-toned — that support illustrations and data visualization. White is used generously for content surfaces to balance the energetic yellow. The rule: yellow must appear on every surface in some form, but it rarely dominates informational screens — a header bar, a button, an accent rule, an illustration detail. Dark mode inverts to a dark background with yellow as the luminous accent.

## Typography approach
Mailchimp uses a distinctive brand serif (a custom or carefully chosen display serif — Georgia-esque but sharper) for headlines and marketing moments, paired with a clean sans-serif (Graphik or similar) for UI. The serif introduces warmth and character; the sans-serif keeps the interface legible and efficient. UI type runs at 14–16px — slightly larger than average — to feel accessible and easy. Line-height is generous (1.5–1.6) to create an airy, approachable reading experience. The serif/sans-serif pairing is the typographic signature — it communicates "we are both creative and capable."

## Spacing & density
Mailchimp uses generous, open layouts that feel more like a well-designed marketing page than a dense admin tool. Cards and panels use 24–32px padding. Section breaks get 48–64px vertical spacing. The audience dashboard, campaign builder, and automation canvas all maintain low information density — one clear task or insight per visual group. This openness is deliberate: it reduces cognitive load for small business owners who may use the tool infrequently and need to re-orient each time. No single screen should feel overwhelming.

## Corner radius & shape language
Soft and friendly throughout: 8–12px on cards, 8px on buttons, 6px on inputs, and fully rounded (pill-shaped) for tags, badges, and toggle elements. The shape language is consistently rounded — no sharp corners anywhere — which reinforces the approachable, non-threatening personality. The campaign builder uses slightly larger rounding (12–16px) on drag-and-drop content blocks that feel tactile and inviting to manipulate. Illustrations use organic, curved shapes exclusively.

## Elevation & depth
Depth is flat-to-subtle. Cards on white backgrounds use very light 1px borders in warm gray rather than shadows. Interactive cards (draggable content blocks, clickable campaign thumbnails) gain a subtle 0px-2px-8px shadow on hover. Modals and dropdowns use a soft shadow with warm undertones — never cold gray. The elevation strategy is minimal because information hierarchy comes from color (yellow draws the eye) and typography (the serif/sans-serif pairing), not from z-depth.

## Iconography & imagery
Icons are friendly and slightly chunky — drawn at 20–24px with 2px strokes and rounded caps. They feel hand-drawn adjacent (though not literally hand-drawn) and sit comfortably next to the illustration style. Mailchimp's illustration system is one of its strongest design signatures: flat-color, geometric, slightly surreal illustrations featuring Freddie the mascot, abstract shapes, and diverse human characters. The illustration palette is warm and cohesive (yellows, peaches, greens, off-black). Illustrations appear everywhere — empty states, loading screens, onboarding flows, upgrade prompts, error pages, and confirmation screens. They transform what could be frustrating moments (an error, an empty list) into moments of brand delight. Never use generic stock photography; custom illustration is essential to the language.

## Signature patterns
The campaign builder with its drag-and-drop content blocks that snap into place with a satisfying motion. The yellow-branded top navigation that persists across every screen as the brand anchor. The audience dashboard with large, friendly data visualizations (donut charts with thick strokes, oversized percentage displays). The step-by-step wizard flows for creating campaigns, automations, and forms — each step is a distinct screen with an illustration, a headline, and a single clear action. The "high five" confirmation moments after completing an action (sending a campaign, importing a list). The toggle-heavy settings panels that use oversized, satisfying switches.

## Motion philosophy
Motion is playful and encouraging, never gratuitous. The drag-and-drop campaign builder uses spring physics (300ms, gentle bounce) when blocks snap into place. Confirmation screens use celebratory motion: a subtle scale-up and fade-in of the success illustration, a bounce on the CTA button. Page transitions are smooth fades (250ms). Toggles animate with a satisfying slide-and-color-change (200ms). Loading states use branded animation — the Freddie mascot or a yellow accent element animating — rather than generic spinners. Every motion should feel like a small reward or a helpful guide, never an interruption.

## Voice & copy tone
Warm, encouraging, and characterful — Mailchimp's copy is perhaps the most distinctive in SaaS. Headlines use the serif typeface and write in a friendly, slightly witty voice: "Find your people," "Make it pop," "Ready, set, send." Instructional copy is clear and assumes no prior knowledge, using plain language instead of marketing jargon. Error messages and empty states are particularly strong: they acknowledge the situation warmly and guide the user forward with a clear next step. Second-person throughout. The occasional pun or playful turn of phrase is welcome but never at the expense of clarity. The voice treats the user as a creative partner, not a novice. Sentence case is used for UI copy; title case is reserved for hero headlines.

## Explicitly do not
- Do not reproduce Mailchimp's logo, wordmark, Freddie mascot, or any trademarked assets.
- Do not copy Mailchimp's UI copy, taglines, or branded messaging verbatim.
- This reference describes a design language to draw inspiration from, not a license to clone Mailchimp's product or visual identity.
