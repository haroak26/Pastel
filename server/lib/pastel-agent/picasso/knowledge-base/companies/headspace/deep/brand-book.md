# Headspace Brand Book — Deep Dive

## Overview

Headspace is "meditation made simple." The brand is built around a single idea: that the
design itself can feel therapeutic. Every pixel, every word, every interaction is crafted
to reduce anxiety and promote calm. This isn't decoration layered on top of a meditation
product — it is the product.

---

## Brand Personality

Headspace feels like a gentle friend who cares about your wellbeing, not a fitness coach
pushing you to do more. The personality sits at the intersection of:

- **Calm** — Never frantic, never urgent, never aggressive
- **Gentle** — Soft edges, soft words, soft interactions
- **Mindful** — Intentional about every detail, nothing is accidental
- **Optimistic** — Warm and hopeful, never saccharine or forced
- **Human** — Real people, real emotions, real struggles acknowledged

The brand doesn't judge. It meets you where you are. It celebrates small wins without making
you feel like you're behind.

---

## Tone of Voice

Headspace speaks like a meditation guide — never like a coach, a doctor, or a self-help guru.

### Character
- Gentle and supportive
- Inviting, not demanding
- Simple and clear
- Warm and human
- Patient and unhurried

### Sentence-level patterns
- "Take a moment" — inviting, an offer not a command
- "Let's try something" — collaborative, never telling
- "If you're feeling..." — acknowledges rather than prescribes
- "Whenever you're ready" — no pressure, no timeline
- Short, calm sentences with generous pauses (commas, line breaks)

### Anti-patterns
- Never pushy ("YOU NEED TO MEDITATE")
- Never guilt-inducing ("You missed a day!")
- Never clinical or cold
- Never uses jargon or esoteric meditation terminology
- Never sarcastic or snarky — sincerity is the foundation

The voice always leaves room for the user to decide. It's a gentle suggestion, never an
instruction. Even error messages feel kind: "Hmm, something's not quite right. Let's try
that again when you're ready."

## Visual Identity

### Philosophy
The visual language of Headspace is deliberately soft, warm, and organic. The design
doesn't compete with the content of meditation — it supports a mental state of calm
before you even press play.

### Core elements

**Color: The Warm Pastel Universe**
Headspace owns the warm pastel palette. Every identifiable Headspace screen feels warm
— soft oranges, gentle corals, muted yellows, blush pinks. These colors activate a
sense of comfort and safety. Cold blues and harsh greens are virtually absent from the
core palette. Even deep focus screens feel warm-toned.

**Organic Shapes: The Blob Language**
Hard geometric shapes feel tense. Headspace uses organic blobs, soft curves, and
rounded forms everywhere. Buttons are pill-shaped. Cards are rounded. Illustrations
feature blob-like characters and landscapes. Nothing feels sharp or angular.

**Illustration: The Friendly World**
Characters are simple, round, and expressive without being childlike. They have soft
features, gentle poses, and warm expressions. Environments use the same organic blob
language — rolling hills, soft clouds, rounded trees. The illustration style is
consistent and immediately recognizable.

**White Space: The Breathing Room**
Space is a design element equal to color and shape. Content never feels crowded.
Between sections, between elements, between lines — there is always room to breathe.
This isn't wasted space; it's intentional calm built into the layout.

**Animation: The Subtle Movement**
Motion reinforces calm. Breathing exercises expand and contract slowly. Page
transitions are soft fades. Scroll-triggered elements glide gently into view.
Nothing pops, bounces, or surprises.

## Typography

Headspace uses rounded, friendly sans-serif typefaces — similar to Nunito or Varela
Round — with a humanist touch:

- **Weights**: Light (300) through semi-bold (600), avoiding heavy bold or thin hairline
- **Body text**: 16-18px for readability on mobile, generous line height (1.6-1.8)
- **Headings**: Gentle size progression, never oversized or aggressive
- **Personality**: Rounded terminals, open apertures, friendly geometry
- **Pairing**: Single typeface family with weight changes for hierarchy — consistency over contrast

---

## Spacing Philosophy

Breathing room is a therapeutic tool, not just a layout decision:

- **Component padding**: 24-48px — generous but not wasteful
- **Section margins**: 64-128px — each section feels like its own moment
- **Card spacing**: 16-24px between cards — each item gets its own space to breathe
- **Content density**: Low — every screen has one primary focus
- **Line spacing**: 1.6-1.8x — slow reading, calm scanning
- **Never**: Stacked elements without clearance, text touching edges, crowded grids

The spacing creates a pacing rhythm. When you scroll, each section arrives as a new
gentle beat, giving the eye and mind time to rest.

## Color Philosophy In Depth

The Headspace palette is built on a foundation of warmth:

### Warm Pastels (Primary)
- Soft orange → energy, warmth, gentle encouragement
- Warm coral → approachability, human connection
- Light yellow → optimism, morning light, new beginnings
- Blush pink → self-care, gentleness, emotional warmth

### Neutrals
- Warm cream → soft backgrounds that feel like paper, not sterile white
- Warm gray → supporting text and UI chrome that doesn't feel cold
- Warm charcoal → the darkest tone, always warmed, never pure #000

### Transitions
- Gradients are soft transitions between warm tones (orange to yellow, coral to pink)
- Never use cold-to-warm transitions
- Gradients are atmospheric, never showy

### Dark Mode
- Backgrounds are warm dark tones — deep browns and warm grays
- Pure black (#000) is never used
- Accent colors shift slightly warmer
- Text on dark backgrounds maintains the 1.6+ line height for readability

### The Anti-Palette
Things Headspace never uses:
- Pure black or pure white
- Saturated primary colors (especially cool blues, greens, reds)
- Neon accents
- High-contrast color pairings
- Cool grays with blue undertones
- Anything that feels "corporate" or "clinical"

## Key Design Patterns

### Daily Meditation Pick
The hero of the home screen. A prominent card with an illustration that matches the
session's theme. Shows title, duration, and a gentle call-to-action ("Start" or "Begin").
The card features soft shadow and generous padding.

### Progress Tracking
- **Streak counter**: A fire emoji with number of consecutive days, displayed warmly
- **Minutes meditated**: Total lifetime minutes, presented as a gentle stat
- **Progress ring**: Circular, warm accent color, shows daily/weekly progress
- **Encouraging messages**: "Nice work!" or "You're building a habit" — never "Don't break the streak!"

### Course Paths
A sequence of sessions presented as a journey. Each step shows a small illustration,
title, and completion status. Active sessions glow warmly. Completed sessions get a
gentle checkmark. The path scrolls horizontally on mobile.

### Sleep Sounds
A grid of atmospheric soundscapes (rain, ocean, wind, etc.). Each soundscape has its
own warm-toned illustration or ambient color background. Large touch targets for
nighttime use. The night mode UI dims everything to warm dark tones.

### Mood Check-In
An emoji grid — simple, one-tap, no friction. User taps how they're feeling and
Headspace offers a relevant session. No typing required. The mood history is visualized
as gentle color blocks over time.

### Breathing Exercise
A full-screen calming experience. An animated circle expands and contracts at a
meditative pace (4 seconds in, 4 seconds out). Subtle ambient sound. Nothing else on
screen. The circle uses warm, slightly glowing colors.

## What Makes Headspace Headspace

When someone sees a Headspace screen, they should feel it before they recognize it.
The design activates a calming response independently of content:

- The warmth of the color palette signals safety
- The organic shapes signal approachability
- The generous space signals there is nothing to rush for
- The gentle motion signals a slower pace of being
- The friendly illustrations signal this is for humans, not experts

Headspace doesn't look designed. It looks cared for.

---

## Signature Moves Checklist

If you're designing something "Headspace-inspired," these are the non-negotiable markers:

1.  Warm pastel palette — if the design could be described as "cool" or "sharp," it's wrong
2.  Organic blob shapes — no rectangles, no sharp corners
3.  Friendly character illustrations — simple, round, warm
4.  Gentle animations — at least 300ms, easing curves that feel like breathing
5.  Breathing exercise circles — the iconic expanding/contracting ring
6.  Streak counter with encouraging messages — "You're doing great," not "Don't miss a day"
7.  Mood tracking with emoji — one-tap, no friction, human emotion
8.  Generous breathing room — negative space is a feature, not a bug
9.  Rounded everything — buttons, cards, containers, all of it
10. Soft shadows — subtle, warm, barely there, used only when needed for depth

## Emotional Experience Map

The user's emotional journey through Headspace follows a deliberate arc:

1.  **Arrival** — Warm, welcoming, no immediate demands. Greeting + daily pick invites gently.
2.  **Exploration** — Browse feels like wandering through a calm space. Content reveals itself.
3.  **Engagement** — Session begins. UI recedes. Full-screen breathing or ambient illustration.
4.  **Completion** — Gentle celebration. "Nice work." Stats updated. Suggested next session.
5.  **Reflection** — Profile shows the journey. Mood history. Minutes. Not a leaderboard — a journal.

Each transition is designed to maintain or deepen calm. There's never a moment where the
design creates tension or urgency.

## Competitive Context

Headspace defined the "calm tech" category. Competitors like Calm lean into nature
imagery and soundscapes; others like Balance take a more structured coaching approach.
Headspace's differentiator is that the *design itself* does therapeutic work — the
brand doesn't just package meditation content, it embodies a meditative quality in
every visual and interaction choice.

This is the key insight for any Headspace-inspired design: **you should feel calmer
looking at the design, even before you start meditating.** That's the bar.
