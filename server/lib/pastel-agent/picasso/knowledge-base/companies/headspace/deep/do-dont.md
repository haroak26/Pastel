# Headspace Design — Do & Don't Reference

## Core Principle

Headspace design is therapeutic. Every visual and interaction choice either contributes
to a sense of calm or works against it. When in doubt, choose softer, warmer, slower,
and more spacious.

---

## Color

### DO

- ✅ Use warm pastels as the dominant palette: soft orange, warm coral, light yellow, blush pink
- ✅ Use warm cream or very light warm gray for backgrounds — never pure white
- ✅ Use warm charcoal for the darkest tones — never pure black (#000)
- ✅ Create gradients only between two warm tones (orange → yellow, coral → pink)
- ✅ Keep saturation low — colors should feel soft and gentle, never loud
- ✅ Use warm-toned semantic colors: muted green for success, warm amber for caution, warm red for errors
- ✅ In dark mode, use warm dark tones (deep brown, warm charcoal) — never cold grays or pure black

### DON'T

- ❌ Never use cold blues, cool grays, or mint greens as primary colors
- ❌ Never use pure white (#FFF) or pure black (#000) as backgrounds
- ❌ Never use saturated, vibrant, or neon colors
- ❌ Never create gradients with cold-to-warm transitions (blue → orange)
- ❌ Never use high-contrast color pairings that create visual tension
- ❌ Never use cool gray with blue undertones for text or UI chrome
- ❌ Never use alarm red for error states — use warm, muted alternatives

---

## Shape & Border Radius

### DO

- ✅ Use organic, blob-like shapes for illustrations and decorative elements
- ✅ Apply generous border radius: minimum 8px on everything, 12-24px on cards
- ✅ Use pill shapes (border-radius: 9999px) for buttons, tags, and chips
- ✅ Use irregular, flowing curves for blob illustrations — avoid geometric precision
- ✅ Round profile avatars with soft, warm borders

### DON'T

- ❌ Never use sharp corners or square edges (0px, 2px, 4px radius)
- ❌ Never use geometric, angular shapes for decorative elements
- ❌ Never use hard-edged rectangles for cards or containers
- ❌ Never use perfectly circular or symmetrical shapes that feel mechanical
- ❌ Never let sharp or angular illustration styles enter the visual language

---

## Spacing & Layout

### DO

- ✅ Maintain generous breathing room: 24-48px component padding, 64-128px section margins
- ✅ Keep content density low — each screen should have one primary focus
- ✅ Use 16-24px gaps between cards in lists and grids
- ✅ Apply 24px+ horizontal screen padding on all sides
- ✅ Cap text line length at ~72 characters for calm reading pace
- ✅ Use whitespace as a deliberate design element — it's not empty, it's breathing room
- ✅ Let each section feel like its own moment — space between sections should be substantial

### DON'T

- ❌ Never crowd content — if there's more than one primary call-to-action, simplify
- ❌ Never stack elements without generous clearance between them
- ❌ Never let text or UI elements touch screen edges
- ❌ Never use tight or compact layouts in primary content areas
- ❌ Never stack more than 2-3 cards in a vertical list without section breaks
- ❌ Never justify text to create rigid blocks — ragged right is friendlier

---

## Typography

### DO

- ✅ Use rounded, friendly sans-serif typefaces (Nunito, Varela Round, or similar)
- ✅ Use light to semi-bold weights (300-600) — never heavy bold
- ✅ Set body text at 16-18px with generous line height (1.6-1.8x)
- ✅ Use weight changes for hierarchy: light for hero numbers, regular for body, medium for emphasis
- ✅ Keep headings modest — gentle size progression, never oversized or aggressive
- ✅ Use sentence case for headings and labels — Title Case feels formal and tense

### DON'T

- ❌ Never use bold (700+) weights — they feel harsh and demanding
- ❌ Never use thin/hairline weights (100) — they feel fragile and anxiety-inducing
- ❌ Never use condensed or narrow typefaces that reduce readability
- ❌ Never set body text below 14px on mobile
- ❌ Never use all-caps for labels or CTAs (pill buttons are the exception, and even then, prefer sentence case)
- ❌ Never use monospace or slab-serif typefaces that feel technical or formal

---

## Shadows & Elevation

### DO

- ✅ Use very subtle, soft shadows with warm undertones
- ✅ Use generous blur radius (12-32px) to keep shadows diffuse and gentle
- ✅ Keep shadow opacity low (0.06-0.10)
- ✅ Use warm brown tones in shadow colors, not black or cool gray
- ✅ Use shadows only to create depth — not as decoration
- ✅ Use surface lightness changes as a primary elevation cue (especially in dark mode)

### DON'T

- ❌ Never use hard, sharp shadows (low blur radius, high opacity)
- ❌ Never use colored glow shadows (green glow, blue glow) for elevation
- ❌ Never use drop shadows with 0px blur — they feel like cut paper
- ❌ Never stack multiple harsh shadows on one element
- ❌ Never use dark overlay shadows that create dramatic depth — keep it airy

---

## Animation & Motion

### DO

- ✅ Use slow, gentle transitions: 300-500ms for UI, 4000ms+ for breathing animations
- ✅ Use smooth easing curves — ease-out and ease-in-out as defaults
- ✅ Make page transitions feel like gentle cross-fades, not slides or pushes
- ✅ Animate breathing exercises with slow, meditative expansion and contraction
- ✅ Respect prefers-reduced-motion — make transitions even slower or instant with fade
- ✅ Use scroll-triggered reveals that glide elements gently into view (500-700ms)
- ✅ Keep progress indicator animations slow and satisfying (800ms+ for ring fills)

### DON'T

- ❌ Never use fast animations (under 200ms) — they create urgency and tension
- ❌ Never use spring physics or bouncy easings — they feel playful, not calming
- ❌ Never use aggressive page transitions (slides, pushes, flips)
- ❌ Never animate elements with sudden appearances — always fade or gently reveal
- ❌ Never use loading spinners that spin fast — slow rotating or pulsing indicators only
- ❌ Never autoplay videos or animations without a gentle fade-in
- ❌ Never use parallax scrolling effects that create visual dissonance

---

## Illustration

### DO

- ✅ Use warm, soft, blob-like character designs with simple, friendly expressions
- ✅ Use organic, flowing shapes for environments (rounded hills, soft clouds, blob trees)
- ✅ Keep illustrations simple and clear — not detailed or complex
- ✅ Use warm pastel fills consistent with the brand palette
- ✅ Keep character expressions gentle and serene — closed or softly open eyes
- ✅ Include diverse representation in age, skin tone, and presentation

### DON'T

- ❌ Never use sharp, angular, or geometric illustration styles
- ❌ Never use highly detailed or realistic illustrations — they feel complex, not calming
- ❌ Never use cold or saturated colors in illustrations
- ❌ Never use characters with intense, aggressive, or overly excited expressions
- ❌ Never use isometric or technical illustration styles
- ❌ Never use thin-line or wireframe illustration approaches — keep shapes filled and warm

---

## UI Components

### DO

- ✅ Use pill-shaped buttons with generous padding (16-24px horizontal)
- ✅ Design cards with soft rounded corners, warm backgrounds, and subtle shadows
- ✅ Keep form inputs simple and rounded with soft borders
- ✅ Use bottom tab bars with rounded top corners and warm styling
- ✅ Make touch targets generous (minimum 44x44px, preferably 48x48px)
- ✅ Use progress rings with warm accent colors and slow-fill animations
- ✅ Use emoji for mood selection — they're universally friendly and human

### DON'T

- ❌ Never use sharp rectangular buttons — always round them significantly
- ❌ Never design complex, multi-step forms with many fields
- ❌ Never use aggressive toggle switches with harsh snap animations
- ❌ Never build dense data tables — if you need data, use stat cards
- ❌ Never use checkboxes or radio buttons with sharp corners
- ❌ Never use loading skeletons with sharp, jerky animations
- ❌ Never use tooltips that appear suddenly — they should fade in gently

---

## Tone & Copy

### DO

- ✅ Speak gently and supportively — like a meditation guide, not a coach
- ✅ Use invitational language: "Take a moment," "Whenever you're ready," "Let's try"
- ✅ Keep sentences short, calm, and clear
- ✅ Acknowledge emotions without judgment: "If you're feeling anxious..."
- ✅ Celebrate progress without pressure: "Nice work!" not "Don't break your streak!"
- ✅ Use the user's name to create warmth and personal connection
- ✅ Make error messages kind: "Hmm, something's not quite right. Let's try again."

### DON'T

- ❌ Never use pushy, commanding, or demanding language
- ❌ Never guilt the user: no "You missed a day!" or "You're falling behind!"
- ❌ Never use jargon or esoteric meditation terminology
- ❌ Never use clinical or medical language that feels diagnostic
- ❌ Never use sarcasm, snark, or witty copy — sincerity is the foundation
- ❌ Never create FOMO through copy: no "Limited time!" or "Don't miss out!"
- ❌ Never use all-caps anywhere except possibly in legal disclaimers

---

## Navigation & Architecture

### DO

- ✅ Keep navigation simple: 4-5 primary tabs maximum
- ✅ Use bottom tab bar as primary mobile navigation
- ✅ Make the home screen a warm welcome, not a dashboard of tasks
- ✅ Let users explore at their own pace — don't push them toward specific content
- ✅ Use progressive disclosure: reveal more content on scroll, not all at once

### DON'T

- ❌ Never use hamburger menus as the only navigation on mobile
- ❌ Never create deep navigation hierarchies that require many taps
- ❌ Never use onboarding flows that feel like interrogations (long signup, preference quizzes)
- ❌ Never use notification badges on the app icon — no pending-task anxiety
- ❌ Never push upsells or premium features aggressively

---

## The Golden Rule

If a visual or interaction choice could be described with words like:
- "Sharp" → wrong
- "Fast" → wrong
- "Cool" (temperature) → wrong
- "Bold" → wrong
- "Loud" → wrong
- "Aggressive" → wrong
- "Dense" → wrong
- "Clinical" → wrong

Instead, aim for:
- "Soft" → right
- "Slow" → right
- "Warm" → right
- "Gentle" → right
- "Quiet" → right
- "Inviting" → right
- "Spacious" → right
- "Human" → right

When you look at a Headspace-inspired screen, you should physically feel your
shoulders relax. If they don't, something is wrong.
