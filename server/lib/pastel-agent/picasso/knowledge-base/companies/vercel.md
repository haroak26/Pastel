# Vercel design language
dark-mode-first, near-monochrome black/white with a single sharp accent used sparingly; tight, precise spacing; small radii (4–8px); minimal shadows, relies on borders and contrast for separation over elevation; geometric grotesk sans with monospace accents for technical credibility; dense-but-organized dashboards; terse, confident copy. Fits: developer tools, infrastructure, technical B2B/SaaS.

## When to reach for this reference
Use Vercel design language when designing dashboards, developer tooling UIs, CLI documentation sites, infrastructure consoles, or any technical B2B product targeting developers and engineering leaders. Apply it when the brand needs to signal precision, performance, and understated confidence rather than playfulness or consumer warmth. Reach for this reference when the product is dark-mode-first with sparse color, when data density matters but must stay scannable, and when the audience expects speed and clarity without decorative fluff.

## Brand personality
Precise, confident, and quietly opinionated. Vercel speaks like a senior engineer who knows the answer but doesn't need to prove it. The voice is technical without being gatekeep-y, minimalist without feeling empty. There is an undercurrent of speed: everything feels fast, responsive, and intentional. The brand does not shout; it ships. Visual restraint conveys reliability. The personality avoids trend-chasing, whimsy, or anything that feels like marketing fluff. It earns trust through clarity, not persuasion.

## Color philosophy
Dark-mode-first with a near-monochrome foundation. Backgrounds sit in deep charcoals (#0A0A0A, #111111, #1A1A1A) layering up to lighter surfaces (#222222, #2A2A2A). Text runs white-to-light-gray (#FFFFFF, #E5E5E5, #888888). A single sharp accent—Vercel uses white or a distinctive highlight (their signature geometric purple/magenta-pink, roughly #FF0080 or brand purple)—is used sparingly: active states, primary CTAs, selected items, focus rings. No secondary accent palette. No semantic color overload. Success/warning/error states exist as minimal, desaturated indicators (muted green, muted amber, muted red) that don't compete with the monochrome structure. Gradients are geometric and rare—used only in hero moments or branding marks, never in functional UI.

## Typography approach
Geometric grotesk sans-serif for all core UI: Inter, Geist, or SF Pro. Body text at 13–14px with tight line-height (1.4). Headings at 16–24px with letter-spacing of -0.01em to -0.03em. Monospace (JetBrains Mono, Menlo, or Geist Mono) reserved for code blocks, inline code, log outputs, commit hashes, and any technical data display. Tabular figures for numeric columns. No serifs. No display faces. No decorative typography. Font weights stay at 400 (regular), 500 (medium), 600 (semibold). Bold (700+) is rare. The hierarchy is established through size and color contrast, not through weight overuse. Text labels are sentence case, never all-caps for navigation.

## Spacing & density
Tight, economical, and consistent. Core spacing scale: 4px, 8px, 12px, 16px, 24px, 32px, 48px. Internal component padding defaults to 8px or 12px. Card padding: 16px. Page gutters: 24px. Section gaps: 32px. Dashboard grids use 16px gaps. No loose, airy layouts. Whitespace is intentional, never wasted. Information-dense screens (tables, logs, deployment lists) use compressed row heights (32–36px) with 8px vertical padding. Forms use 8px label-to-input gaps. The system prefers compact but never cramped: every pixel of padding has a job. Use a 4px baseline grid implicitly.

## Corner radius & shape language
Small, controlled radii: 4px for small elements (buttons, inputs, tags, badges), 6px for cards and panels, 8px for modals and dialogs. Nothing rounded-full except status dots and user avatars. Icons and logos may use the brand's geometric construction (triangular, polygonal, architectural) but functional UI elements stay strictly rectangular or softly rounded. The shape language is architectural: right angles, clean geometry, deliberate asymmetry in brand marks but not in UI chrome. No pill shapes. No bubbly forms. No organic curves.

## Elevation & depth
Minimal shadows, maximum restraint. Elevation is communicated primarily through borders (1px solid, semi-transparent white or black depending on mode) and subtle background-color layering in the monochrome scale. When shadows are used, they are tight: 0px 2px 4px with 8% opacity, or 0px 4px 8px with 12% opacity for higher surfaces. Drop shadows never spread wide, never use saturated color, never feel "glowy." Overlays and modals use a semi-transparent backdrop (#000000 at 40–60% opacity) rather than a heavy blur. Depth is implied through content organization and subtle surface color shifts, not through skeuomorphic layering. Z-index hierarchy is flat: two, maybe three levels.

## Iconography & imagery
Geometric, monoline, 1.5–2px stroke weight icons at 16px, 20px, and 24px sizes. Use Lucide, Feather, or Geist Icons. Always stroke-based, never filled unless it's a micro-indicator (status dot, badge count). Icons are monochrome, inheriting the text color of their context. No multi-color icons in UI chrome. Imagery is sparse: product screenshots and architectural diagrams rendered in dark-mode with sharp edges, no overlaid gradients, no stock photography. Background textures, when used, are subtle grid patterns or geometric noise at very low opacity. Hero visuals use the brand's geometric brand language—intersecting planes, wireframe globes, layered translucent shapes—rendered in the accent color over black.

## Signature patterns
Card grids of 2–4 columns with sharp 1px borders, no shadows, tight internal padding. Terminal-style inline code snippets embedded directly in prose or table cells with monospace font and slight background highlight. Top-nav minimal: logo left, sparse nav items (4–6 max), single CTA right. Inline status indicators as small filled circles (green, amber, red) next to monospace labels. Data tables with monospace values, right-aligned numbers, subtle zebra striping via 2% brightness difference, and compact row heights. "Command palette" search bars with monospace placeholder text. File-tree-style sidebars with 1px indentation guides. Keyboard shortcut hints rendered as tiny monospace `<kbd>`-style badges (4px radius, subtle border). Skeleton loaders as pulsing monochrome bars, no color gradients.

## Motion philosophy
Fast, functional, friction-minimized. Transitions run at 150–200ms for micro-interactions (hover, focus, toggle), 200–300ms for enter/exit (modals, dropdowns, page transitions). Easing uses ease-out for entrance (accelerate into position) and ease-in for exit (decelerate out). No spring physics, no bounce, no overshoot—motion is mechanical and predictable. Page transitions are instant or near-instant. Loading states use skeleton screens or minimal determinate progress bars rather than spinners. Hover states are immediate (0ms delay). The goal is perceived speed: nothing should wait on animation to complete before the user can act. Motion serves the data, never the other way around.

## Voice & copy tone
Terse, confident, and technically precise. Sentence case everywhere. No exclamation marks. No "please" or "sorry." No marketing adjectives ("amazing," "beautiful," "powerful"). Use imperative mood for actions ("Deploy," "Create project," "View logs"). Descriptive labels use noun phrases ("Deployment status," "Environment variables"). Error messages state what happened and what to do next in plain language, never blaming the user. Empty states are direct: "No deployments yet. Push a commit to get started." Tooltips and helper text stay under one line. The voice assumes the user is competent and just needs clarity.

## Explicitly do not
Do not use pastel or soft color palettes. Do not introduce decorative elements, illustrations, or gratuitous imagery. Do not use large border radii (>8px) on any functional element. Do not add box shadows to cards or containers as a primary depth cue. Do not use serif fonts, display fonts, or handwritten type. Do not use emojis in UI copy. Do not use animated background effects, particle systems, or parallax scrolling outside deliberate brand moments. Do not create whitespace-heavy, low-density layouts. Do not use colorful badge palettes—one accent, monochrome everything else. Do not design light-mode-first; always start in dark mode and derive light mode as a cautious inversion. Do not use gradient fills on buttons, cards, or functional UI. Do not use backdrop-blur on overlays unless absolutely necessary—prefer semi-transparent dark fill. Do not make the user feel marketed to. Do not decorate. Ship.
