# Spotify Brand Book — Deep Reference

## Brand Personality

**Bold. Cultural. Immersive.**

Spotify is the "audio platform for everyone" — but it doesn't feel generic. It feels like a record store curated by someone with impeccable taste. Every screen is a statement: music is culture, and culture is visual.

The brand operates in darkness. While every other major tech brand builds on white canvases, Spotify went the opposite direction — a dark mode-only identity that makes album art explode with color and makes the listening experience feel immersive, intimate, and premium.

### The Darkness Strategy

Spotify's dark canvas is not just an aesthetic choice — it's a strategic design decision:
- **Album art pops**: On a black background, every album cover looks like a gallery piece
- **Focus on music**: The UI recedes into darkness; the content (music, podcasts) shines
- **Battery-friendly**: Dark pixels save power on OLED screens during long listening sessions
- **Nighttime companion**: Music is often consumed at night — dark UI doesn't disrupt sleep rhythms
- **Premium feel**: Black backgrounds with green accent = sophisticated, not generic

### Emotional Spectrum

- **Discovering new music**: Excitement, curiosity, the thrill of finding something new
- **Listening**: Immersion, focus, mood-matching
- **Sharing**: Connection, cultural identity ("this is my taste")
- **Wrapped (year-end)**: Nostalgia, pride, surprise, shareability

### Brand Attributes (In Priority Order)

1. **Bold** — Large typography, confident design choices, unapologetic darkness
2. **Cultural** — References artists, genres, moments. Feels plugged into the zeitgeist.
3. **Immersive** — The UI gets out of your way. Music is the experience.
4. **Personal** — "Made for You," personalized playlists, Wrapped. Feels like it knows you.
5. **Inclusive** — "Music for everyone." Genre-agnostic, globally accessible.

## Tone of Voice

### Core Principles

- **Bold and confident** — Spotify has cultural authority and uses it
- **Occasionally playful** — playlists have whimsical names ("Songs to Sing in the Shower")
- **Cultural references** — artist quotes, genre jargon, music insider language
- **Inclusive** — speaks to all genres, all cultures, all listening preferences
- **Data-savvy** — comfortable referencing listening stats and personalized data (Wrapped)
- **Never corporate** — no "solutions" or "synergies." This is music, not enterprise software.

### Copy Patterns

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| Playlist names | Creative, playful, specific | "Morning Commute," "Feel Good Friday," "Lorem" |
| Home greeting | Personal, time-aware | "Good evening" / "Good morning, Sarah" |
| Recommendations | Confident, curated | "Made for you" / "Based on your listening" |
| Artist pages | Respectful, informed | Artist bio with cultural context |
| Wrapped | Data-savvy, playful | "You listened to 847 genres this year" |
| Error states | Light, human | "Something went wrong. Try again?" |
| Podcast descriptions | Informative, engaging | Show summary, host info |

### Words They Use

"listen" "play" "discover" "made for you" "recommended" "based on" "your"
"vibe" "mood" "genre" "artist" "album" "track" "episode" "playlist" "mix"

### Words They Avoid

"stream" (too technical, use "play") "consume" (too clinical) "user acquisition"
"retention" "monetize" — anything that sounds like it came from a boardroom

## Visual Identity

### Dark Mode Only

Spotify is the only major consumer app with a dark-only default. There is no light mode. This is a radical design decision that defines the entire visual identity:

- Background: Near-black (#121212) or pure black (#000000) depending on the surface
- Cards: Slightly lighter dark (#181818, #282828) for elevation
- Text: White primary (#FFFFFF), light gray secondary (#B3B3B3)
- Everything is designed to be seen in darkness

### The Green Accent

Spotify green (#1DB954) is the single accent. It appears sparingly but strategically:
- Primary buttons
- Now Playing progress bar
- Active states (selected tab, active playback indicator)
- Brand logo and app icon
- Links and interactive elements

The green never competes with album art. It's the UI accent that says "this is interactive" without demanding attention.

### Album Art as the Primary Color

Album artwork is Spotify's real color palette. Every screen is dominated by the visual language of music:

- **Home**: Playlist covers, album art in grids
- **Now Playing**: Full-width album art as the hero
- **Library**: Album art thumbnails in lists and grids
- **Search**: Genre tiles with mood-based gradient colors
- **Playlist pages**: Header with blended/gradient treatment of the playlist cover

The UI itself is monochromatic. ALL color comes from content — and that's the point.

### Duotone & Gradient Treatments (Marketing)

In marketing, playlist covers, and branded moments (Wrapped), Spotify uses:
- Duotone effects: Two-color overlays on photography (often green + another color)
- Color gradients: Abstract, flowing blends used for playlists and genre visuals
- Glitch/abstract: Experimental treatments for special events and editorial
- Photography: Artist portraits, concert photography, cultural moments

This is separate from the app UI which stays minimal — these treatments appear on covers, ads, and shareable content.

### Signature Design Elements

1. **Persistent Now Playing bar** — always present at the bottom, your current song is never more than a tap away
2. **Album art everywhere** — every playlist, album, and track is represented visually
3. **Bold, large typography** — headings are statements. Circular font, often in all-caps.
4. **Dark gradient backgrounds** — cards and surfaces use subtle dark gradients for depth
5. **Green progress bar** — in the Now Playing view, the single most iconic UI element
6. **Personalized playlists** — "Discover Weekly," "Release Radar," "Daily Mix" — visually distinct
7. **Canvas video loops** — short looping visuals that replace static album art (artist-provided)
8. **Wrapped** — year-end data story with bold graphics, duotone, and shareable cards

## Typography

Spotify uses a custom typeface: **Circular** (designed by Laurenz Brunner, licensed/distributed by Lineto).

### Circular Characteristics

- Geometric sans-serif with clean, modern lines
- Bold, impactful presence — especially at large sizes
- Optimized for both display (massive headings) and UI (small labels)
- Distinctive rounded forms on certain characters (O, C, G)
- Multiple weights: Light, Book, Medium, Bold, Black

### Typographic Hierarchy

| Element | Weight | Size | Notes |
|---------|--------|------|-------|
| Hero heading | Bold/Black | 48-96px | Massive, impactful, often all-caps |
| Section heading | Bold | 28-36px | Confident, scannable |
| Playlist/card title | Bold | 16-18px | Clear on dark backgrounds |
| Artist name | Book | 14px | Secondary to the track/album name |
| Body text | Book | 14-16px | Legible on dark background |
| Labels/captions | Book | 12px | Light gray (#B3B3B3) |
| Button text | Bold | 14px | All-caps for primary actions |
| Timestamps | Book | 12px | Track duration, progress |

### Typography Principles

- **Bold for impact** — headings are statements, not whispers
- **All-caps for CTAs** — primary buttons use uppercase. This is a signature Spotify move.
- **Generous letter-spacing** — 1-2px on headings, 0.5px on buttons
- **White text on dark** — always high contrast, always legible
- **Large scale range** — from 96px hero text to 10px captions. The range is dramatic.
- **Circular only** — no secondary typeface. One font, many weights.

## Spacing Philosophy

### Two Modes of Density

**Browse Mode (generous)**: Home, Search, Library
- Spacious grids for album art and playlist covers
- 16-24px gaps between cards
- Generous section headers with 32-48px spacing
- Content breathes — discovery is visual and leisurely

**Playback Mode (compact)**: Now Playing, persistent player bar
- Tight, focused layout around the album art
- Controls clustered for reachability
- Information density around the current track

### Spacing Scale

| Context | Spacing |
|---------|---------|
| Page content padding | 16-24px |
| Card grid gaps | 16px |
| Section headers | 32px top, 16px bottom |
| List item spacing | 12-16px |
| Now Playing controls | 24-32px between elements |
| Persistent player | Compact (8-12px internal) |
| Modal/dialog padding | 24px |

## Color Philosophy

### The Dark Canvas System

Every screen is dark. The color hierarchy is:

| Layer | Hex | Usage |
|-------|-----|-------|
| Base background | `#121212` | Main app background |
| Elevated surface | `#181818` | Cards, now playing background |
| More elevated | `#282828` | Active cards, hover states, input fields |
| Highlights | `#333333` | Active/hover on dark elements |
| Borders | Minimal | Mostly borderless, elevation does the work |

### Text on Dark

| Token | Hex | Usage |
|-------|-----|-------|
| Primary text | `#FFFFFF` | Headings, titles, active states |
| Secondary text | `#B3B3B3` | Subtitles, metadata, labels |
| Disabled text | `#727272` | Inactive states |

### Accent (The Green)

| Token | Hex | Usage |
|-------|-----|-------|
| Primary green | `#1DB954` | CTAs, progress, active states, links |
| Green hover | `#1ED760` | Slightly lighter for hover states |

### The Content Color Rule

**UI is monochrome. Content provides all color.** Any non-green, non-grayscale color on screen should come from album art, playlist covers, or artist imagery. The UI itself stays neutral to let the music speak.

## Key Interaction Patterns

### Persistent Now Playing Bar

```
+--------------------------------------------------+
|                                                  |
|                                                  |
|                                                  |
+--------------------------------------------------+
| [Album Art 56px]  Track Name · Artist    [❤] [▶] |
|                    [==== Progress =====]          |
+--------------------------------------------------+
```

- Always visible at the bottom of the screen (mobile) or bottom-left (desktop)
- Shows current track: album art thumbnail, track name, artist
- Play/pause and heart controls
- Thin progress bar showing playback position
- Tap to expand to full Now Playing view
- This is the single most important navigation element — it follows you everywhere

### Playlist Cards

```
+---------------------+
|                     |
|                     |
|    COVER IMAGE      |
|    (square, 1:1)    |
|                     |
|                     |
+---------------------+
| Playlist Name       |
| Playlist Description|
+---------------------+
```

- Square aspect ratio (1:1) for playlist/album covers
- Title: 16px Bold, white, single line with ellipsis
- Description/Artist: 14px Book, gray (#B3B3B3)
- Hover: subtle scale(1.02) + shadow
- Tap navigates to playlist/album detail

### Search Bar

```
+--------------------------------------------------+
|  🔍  What do you want to listen to?               |
+--------------------------------------------------+
```

- Full-width, rounded (8px)
- Background: #282828
- Placeholder text in gray (#B3B3B3)
- On focus: background lightens, green border highlight (desktop)
- Search suggestions appear as user types (artists, albums, tracks)

### Genre/Mood Tiles (Browse)

```
+---------------------+  +---------------------+
|                     |  |                     |
|    GRADIENT BG      |  |    GRADIENT BG      |
|                     |  |                     |
|    [Emoji/Icon]     |  |    [Emoji/Icon]     |
|                     |  |                     |
|    Genre Name       |  |    Genre Name       |
+---------------------+  +---------------------+
```

- Large rounded tiles (12-16px radius)
- Each tile has a unique gradient background color
- Genre/mood name in bold
- Often paired with an emoji or simple icon
- Tap navigates to genre-specific browse/playlist view

## What Makes Spotify, Spotify

- **Dark mode as default** — not an option, the ONLY mode. Radical, distinctive, intentional.
- **Album art as the hero** — every screen is a gallery of music culture
- **Green accent on darkness** — iconic, restrained, never overpowering
- **Bold typography** — Circular font in massive sizes, confident all-caps CTAs
- **Persistent player** — your music follows you through the entire app
- **Personalization as identity** — "Made for You," Discover Weekly, Wrapped
- **Cultural relevance** — feels plugged in, not like a utility

### Anti-Patterns (What Spotify Is NOT)

- NOT light mode (ever)
- NOT colorful UI chrome (color comes from content)
- NOT data-dense or table-heavy (it's visual browsing)
- NOT corporate or blue-tone (like enterprise software)
- NOT cluttered or ad-heavy in the core listening experience
- NOT slow or unresponsive (music playback demands speed)
- NOT text-primary browsing (visual browsing through album art)

## Signature Moves

1. **Dark-only interface** — the most iconic design decision. Immersive, premium, content-forward.
2. **Vibrant green accent on black** — instantly recognizable, minimal, powerful
3. **Persistent bottom player** — music follows you everywhere in the app
4. **Album art as primary visual content** — UI is a frame, art is the picture
5. **Bold, large Circular typography** — all-caps CTAs, dramatic scale range
6. **Wrapped: data as storytelling** — year-end listening stats as bold visual narrative
7. **Canvas: album art as video** — short looping visuals replace static covers
8. **Personalized playlists with unique covers** — algorithmic curation with visual identity
9. **Gradient/duotone marketing aesthetic** — separate from app UI but part of brand visual language

## Brand Evolution Notes

- **2008**: Launched as a desktop app with a dark, tech-forward interface
- **2011-2013**: Mobile apps launch. Green accent introduced. Dark canvas remains.
- **2014-2016**: Shift from library-centric to discovery-centric. "Discover Weekly" (2015) changes everything — personalization becomes the core value prop.
- **2018-2020**: Free tier redesign with simplified navigation. Dark aesthetic refined. Canvas video loops added.
- **2021+**: Podcasts and audiobooks expand the platform beyond music. Wrapped becomes a global cultural event. AI DJ (2023) introduces a new interaction model.
- **Key principle**: Darkness and album art remain constant through every redesign. The canvas evolves but the philosophy doesn't.
